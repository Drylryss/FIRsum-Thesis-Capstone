Term  Definition  
Case  Digest  A  structured  summary  of  a  Philippine  Supreme  Court  decision  is  organized  into  the  key  legal  components  of  the  decision.  In  standard  Philippine  legal  practice,  the  digest  format  includes  Facts,  Issue,  and  Ruling.   
FIR  Framework  (Facts-Issues-Ruling)  The  three-block  structural  output  format  was  adopted  by  this  study.  The  Facts  block  narrates  the  legally  relevant  events,  parties,  and  circumstances.  The  Issues  block  states  the  central  legal  question  the  Court  resolves,  in  interrogative  form.  The  Ruling  block  states  the  Court's  resolution  and  its  legal  basis  drawn  from  the  Civil  Code,  established  doctrine,  or  prior  jurisprudence.  
Ponencia  The  main  opinion  of  the  Philippine  Supreme  Court,  written  by  the  ponente  (the  justice  assigned  to  write  the  decision).  The  ponencia  constitutes  the  binding  ruling  of  the  Court  and

serves  as  the  unit  of  analysis  in  this  study.  Concurring  and  dissenting  opinions  are  excluded.  
Hybrid  Extractive-Abstractive  Summarization  
A  two-stage  summarization  approach  that  combines  extractive  and  abstractive  methods.  Extractive  stages  identify  relevant  source  content,  while  abstractive  stages  generate  coherent  paraphrased  summaries  from  the  selected  information.   
Rhetorical  Role  Classification  The  task  of  assigning  each  sentence  in  a  legal  document  to  one  of  a  set  of  functional  categories  based  on  its  role  in  the  legal  argument.  In  this  study,  the  four  labels  used  are  FACT,  ISSUE,  RULING,  and  OTHER.  This  classification  mechanism  identifies  which  sentences  belong  in  each  FIR  block.  
Human-in-the-Loop  (HITL)  A  validation  mechanism  in  which  a  human  expert  reviews,  corrects,  or  rejects  system  outputs  before  they  are  treated  as  final.  In  this  study,  the  HITL  layer  consists  of  an  attorney-advisor  who  reviews  generated  FIR  summaries  using  a  structured  binary  checklist,  and  an  evaluation  panel  of  student-journalists  and  general  university-level  students  who  assess  perceived  quality  using  a  Likert-scale  instrument.  The  HITL  is  not  a  supplementary  feature  of  the  system;  it  is  a  structural  component  necessitated  by  the  documented  failure  modes  of  language  models  within  legal  contexts.  
Domain  Adaptation  The  process  of  adjusting  a  pre-trained  language  model  to  perform  better  on  a  specific  domain  or  corpus.  In  this  study,  domain  adaptation  takes  two  forms:  LoRA  fine-tuning  of  Legal-BERT  on  annotated  Philippine  SC  sentences,  and  few-shot  prompting  of  Legal-Pegasus  using  attorney-reviewed  Philippine  FIR  digest  exemplars.
